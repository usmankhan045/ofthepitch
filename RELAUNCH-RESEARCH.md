# Of The Pitch — Relaunch Research & Plan

Evidence behind the pivot from World Cup coverage to premium sports travel.
Compiled 3 September 2026.

Organised by **confidence level**, not by topic — because what matters when acting
on this is how much each claim can be trusted.

- **9** verified claims (merchant, government or venue sources)

## The finding that reshapes everything

**Where the outfit is the product, a retailer already ranks.**

M&S, Charles Tyrwhitt and Slater Menswear own the men's raceday queries. Hospitality
resellers and fascinator retailers own "what not to wear." These merchants publish
outfit content because it sells clothes, and they have the domain authority to hold
the spot.

The defensible position is the accuracy those merchants *can't* publish — wrapped
around the logistics they don't cover. No retailer will tell you Wimbledon has no
spectator dress code, because that fact destroys their reason for writing the article.

---

## VERIFIED — safe to plan against

### UK ticket resale above face value is being banned
**Source:** [gov.uk announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-bans-ticket-touting-to-protect-fans-from-rip-off-prices)

Announced 19 Nov 2025: reselling above face value plus unavoidable fees becomes
illegal, with a legal duty on platforms to monitor and enforce, and CMA penalties up
to 10% of global turnover. **Not yet in force** — requires primary legislation
"when parliamentary time allows."

> **Consequence:** commission on the mark-up *is* the resale business model. Cut it
> for UK/EU traffic. Viagogo's CMA enforcement history makes it a brand-safety
> problem for an independence-positioned site regardless.

### Wimbledon has no spectator dress code for grounds admission
**Source:** [wimbledon.com general information](https://www.wimbledon.com/en_GB/visit/general_information)

Official guidance is to dress comfortably and prepare for British weather, plus a
bag limit of 40×30×30cm. Dress codes exist only for Members, Debenture holders and
hospitality areas — not for a grounds pass.

The widespread belief in a strict code conflates three separate things: the players'
all-white rule, the Members' code, and the Royal Box. For an ordinary ticket holder,
none apply.

> **Why it matters:** this is a genuinely useful correction that no retailer will
> make. That asymmetry is your opening.

### Royal Ascot publishes genuinely detailed, enforced rules
**Source:** [ascot.com Royal Enclosure dress code](https://www.ascot.com/royal-ascot/what-to-wear-royal-enclosure)

Separate pages per enclosure, split by ladies and gentlemen, plus a dedicated FAQ
answering the exact edge cases people worry about — whether a hat is required, what
counts as a sheer strap, whether a short-sleeved shirt works with morning dress.

> **Consequence:** real complexity worth explaining properly. This is the pilot piece.

### Premium hospitality runs no public affiliate programs
Wimbledon is exclusive to Keith Prowse (40-year relationship). F1 Paddock Club sells
through authorised agents — F1 Experiences, Quint, Engage — none with public programs.
Ascot and Cheltenham the same.

> **Consequence:** the four-figure clicks are real, but they need a negotiated B2B
> partnership and a media kit, not a signup form. That's a year-two play. It pushes
> apparel and travel adjacency to the front of the revenue model.

### Occasionwear rates are invisible without an Awin account
John Lewis, Selfridges and Hatshopping.co.uk are confirmed on the network; none are
publicly priced. Awin also charges publishers a 3.5% tracking fee on top of commission.

> **Consequence:** an Awin publisher account gates your entire outfit vertical.
> Apply *before* writing, not after.

## DISPROVEN

Both of these were my assertions, offered before I checked them. Each would have sent
real work in the wrong direction.

### ~~"Men's spectator outfit content is underserved"~~ — WRONG
Page one for men's raceday outfits is Marks & Spencer, Charles Tyrwhitt, Slater
Menswear, Upscale Menswear, NYRA and America's Best Racing. Menswear brands have
every commercial incentive to publish this, and they have.

**Averted:** doubling outfit output against M&S's domain authority.

### ~~"What NOT to wear consistently outperforms"~~ — WRONG
It's the first thing every retailer and hospitality reseller writes. The SERP is a
tailor's blog, two hospitality resellers, a fascinator retailer, Forbes, Horse & Hound
and assorted content farms.

**Averted:** building a content pillar on a crowded, merchant-owned query.

## The one rule that keeps this honest

**The advice has to be identical whether or not anyone pays you.** Recommend what's
correct, then link it — never reverse-engineer the styling from what commissions best.
Readers detect the difference, and Google's helpful-content systems are specifically
tuned for it.

Your existing affiliate disclosure page and independent-guide positioning are assets.
Content that reads as a storefront burns them.

# Pinterest Research — outbound clicks

Added 3 September 2026. The revenue model depends on outbound clicks, so this was
researched separately.

## The headline: nobody knows

**No published A/B test exists** comparing "answer given away on the pin" vs "answer
withheld," measuring saves and outbound clicks separately. Not from publishers, tool
vendors, agencies or academia. Any advice on this — including mine, in both directions
— is reasoning, not evidence.

**No credible outbound CTR benchmark exists either.** The "0.2–0.5% CTR" and "2–5%
engagement rate" figures circulating widely trace to a cluster of AI-generated stat
sites citing each other with no origin study; several are dated into the future. Even
Sprout Social carries the 2–5% figure with no methodology. Eight domains, zero
independent sources.

> **Opportunity:** every competing page recycles unsourced numbers. Running this test
> properly on our own pins — holding board, timing and keyword constant, logging
> impressions/saves/outbound clicks per variant — would produce the only real data
> that exists on it.

## Tier 1 — Pinterest's own documentation

### Pinterest hides the "Visit site" button under specific conditions
**Source:** [Pinterest Create, 16 Apr 2025](https://create.pinterest.com/blog/pin-link-update/)

The button moves into the "…" dropdown when:
- the link 404s
- **the pin content doesn't match the landing page**
- the page loads slowly — they name **sub-4-second load time**
- the page is unsafe

> **Consequence:** a pin promising what the page doesn't deliver loses the click
> affordance mechanically. This is the documented argument against curiosity-gap pins,
> stronger than the policy argument. The load-time requirement also favours our
> Next.js build over the old WordPress install.

### Curiosity-gap clickbait is against policy
**Source:** [Community guidelines](https://policy.pinterest.com/en/community-guidelines) · [Enforcement](https://policy.pinterest.com/en/enforcement)

Pinterest prohibits "sensational clickbait tactics that exploit user curiosity."
Penalty is **limited distribution** — the pin stays live by direct link but disappears
from search and the home feed.

### Saves drive distribution
**Source:** [Pin performance and distribution](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pin-performance-and-distribution)

Saves are the strongest engagement metric, and engagement signals determine
distribution. So saves are not in competition with clicks — they buy the impressions
that produce clicks.

> **Caveat worth holding:** Pinterest's organic guidance optimises for saves, and its
> click guidance sits almost entirely in ads documentation. Pinterest benefits when
> users stay on the platform. Not a neutral source on this specific question.

### Official pin specs
| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | **2:3, 1000×1500px** — larger ratios get cut off in feeds |
| Pin title | 100 chars, but **only the first ~40 reliably show** — front-load the hook |
| Description | 500 chars |
| Format | PNG or JPEG |
| Cadence | Original content **weekly** (no numeric daily limit is published) |

### Duplicate pins are discouraged
"Try not to repeatedly save the same Pins or upload content that already exists on
Pinterest. You may get flagged as spam and get temporarily blocked from creating Pins."

Applies to duplicate **creative**, not duplicate destination URLs. Multiple genuinely
distinct pins per article is neither prohibited nor endorsed — Pinterest is silent.
Each pin must be real separate creative, not a recolour.

### Idea Pins no longer exist as a separate format
**Source:** [Pinterest newsroom, 10 May 2023](https://newsroom.pinterest.com/news/introducing-an-easier-way-to-create-and-inspire-on-pinterest/)

All formats unified under "Pins" and **all organic pins now support destination links.**
Any advice repeating "Idea Pins can't link" is pre-May-2023.

## Tier 2 — the one solid measured number

**Pins with alt text earned 123% more outbound clicks**, 25% more impressions and 56%
more profile visits.
**Source:** [Tailwind 2025 Benchmark Report Pt.2](https://www.tailwindapp.com/pinterest-marketing/research/2025-benchmark-study-part-2) — 17,000 accounts, 1.2M+ pins, 90-day windows ending Oct 2024, paid pins excluded. Vendor-run, so discount somewhat; still the only properly sourced figure in the space.

> **Act on it:** write real alt text on every pin. Cheapest available lift.

## Where Pinterest publishes nothing

Clicks-vs-saves guidance for organic pins · whether text overlay affects clicks ·
whether Rich Pins improve CTR · any daily pin limit · multiple pins per URL ·
organic outbound-click benchmarks.

These six gaps are exactly where third-party SEO blogs are loudest and least sourced.
The "Rich Pins boost CTR by X%" claim has no first-party basis at all.

## The defensible working position

1. **Match the pin to the page exactly** — documented penalty otherwise
2. **Alt text on everything** — the one measured lift available
3. **2:3, 1000×1500, hook in the first 40 characters**
4. **Genuinely distinct creative per pin**, never recolours
5. **Test the save-vs-click question ourselves** — trust no one on it, including me

## Source pollution warning

These domains publish confident Pinterest benchmarks with no methodology, no sample
size and no named data collection, citing each other in a circle:
`sociavault.com` · `gensumo.com` · `posteverywhere.ai` · `socialrails.com` ·
`outfy.com` · `pinfreshly.com` · `madpinmedia.com` · `84pins.com` · `pintzy.tools` ·
`apaya.com` · `iqfluence.io` · `webfx.com`

Reject all. If a Pinterest statistic appears without a traceable origin study, it is
almost certainly from this cluster.
