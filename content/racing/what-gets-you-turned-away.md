Most people worry about the wrong thing. The rules that actually stop people at a British racecourse gate are not about being underdressed, they are about specific banned items, the wrong enclosure, and where you bought your ticket. At Royal Ascot, four things are banned everywhere on site regardless of which enclosure you are in: trainers, denim, leggings and shorts.

This is a guide to what genuinely gets refused, based on what Ascot and the Jockey Club publish. Most of it is easier to comply with than people expect.

## What is banned in every enclosure at Royal Ascot?

Four items are prohibited across the entire site, whichever ticket you hold: trainers, denim, leggings and shorts.

On top of that, fancy dress, novelty patterns and fabrics are banned everywhere. Ascot defines that broadly, and it includes slogans, phrases, promotional messaging, brand logos and cartoon imagery.

That last category is wider than most people assume. A shirt with a visible brand logo across the chest is technically caught by it.

## Can you wear a fascinator to Royal Ascot?

Fascinators are permitted in the Queen Anne, Village and Windsor Enclosures. They are not permitted in the Royal Enclosure unless you are under 17.

This is the single most common mistake, because a fascinator is what most people picture when they think of Royal Ascot. In the Royal Enclosure, a hat must be worn, or a headpiece with a solid base of at least 4 inches or 10cm in diameter. Ascot defines a fascinator as a small headpiece attached to a comb, hair clip or headband without that solid base.

The practical version: if the base is smaller than a drinks coaster, it will not pass in the Royal Enclosure. In every other enclosure it is fine, with no size requirement at all.

## Are stag and hen party outfits allowed at the races?

No. Ascot does not allow any items relating to stag and hen parties at Royal Ascot, and treats them as novelty clothing. That covers sashes, badges and tiaras.

Cheltenham handles it differently. The Jockey Club permits fancy dress in most areas, provided the outfit is not offensive, and reserves the right to decline entry. But fancy dress is not permitted in hospitality, restaurant facilities, or the Club Enclosure.

So the same outfit can be fine in one part of a racecourse and refused in another. Check which enclosure your ticket is for, not just which racecourse.

## Do you have to wear socks at Royal Ascot?

Yes. Socks covering the ankle must be worn with full length trousers. This applies in the Royal, Queen Anne and Village Enclosures.

It sounds trivial, and it is the kind of detail that gets missed because no other event enforces it. Ankle socks that disappear into a loafer do not comply.

## Which ties are allowed in which enclosure?

The rules on ties vary by enclosure, and they are not intuitive.

| Enclosure | Necktie | Bow tie | Cravat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Royal | Required | Not permitted | Not permitted |
| Queen Anne | Required | Not permitted | Not permitted |
| Village | Permitted | Permitted | Permitted |
| Windsor | No rules | No rules | No rules |

The trap is buying a bow tie for the Queen Anne Enclosure because it feels more formal. It is not permitted there, while it is perfectly acceptable in the more relaxed Village Enclosure.

## Can you be refused entry over your ticket?

Yes, and this catches more people than any dress rule. Ascot states it enforces the right to refuse entry to anyone who bought a ticket from an unofficial source. It names ticket touts, ticket exchanges such as ViaGogo, and auction sites such as eBay.

A refusal on these grounds means no entry and no refund, because the ticket was never valid to begin with. Buy from the racecourse directly or from an official partner.

Ascot also reserves the right to refuse entry or eject anyone showing signs of intoxication or drug use. Searches on entry and sniffer dogs are both in use, and any sign of intoxication during the day leads to immediate ejection.

## What can you not bring into the racecourse?

At Royal Ascot, food and drink can only be brought into the Windsor Enclosure. Nothing may be brought into the Royal, Queen Anne or Village Enclosures.

Even in the Windsor Enclosure the rules are specific. One bottle of sparkling wine or champagne per person over 18, brought in a hamper or cool bag, at the time of first entry. Beer, cider, spirits, fortified wines and bottles of Pimm's are all refused. Soft drinks are only allowed in with the seals intact, and sharp bladed kitchen knives are not permitted.

Groups get caught by one particular rule. If the food and champagne are split unevenly between hampers, everyone should arrive together to be searched. Anyone arriving with more alcohol than food has to dispose of the excess at the gate.

Nothing can be brought in on re-entry. If you leave and come back, you come back empty handed.

## What is allowed that people assume is not?

Several things that sound risky are explicitly fine, and Ascot has published clarifications on them.

- **Open backed dresses and tops.** Permitted, as long as the garment is not strapless, off the shoulder or bardot.
- **Culottes.** Treated as a dress, so the length and strap rules apply, but they are allowed.
- **Lace, tulle, voile and organza.** All permitted. The ban on sheer fabric applies to see through or entirely transparent material, not to these.
- **Trouser suits and jumpsuits.** Both fine in every enclosure. A trouser suit needs matching material and colour, and a jumpsuit must fall below the knee.
- **National dress.** Overseas visitors may wear the formal national dress of their country. If it does not include a hat, none is needed, and religious headdress is accepted in place of a hat.
- **Service dress.** Serving military personnel may wear Service Dress or equivalent.

## What if a medical condition stops you following the dress code?

Both racecourses make provision for this. Ascot asks that you contact its access officer at accessibility@ascot.com in advance, setting out the issue, and states it will try to accommodate wherever possible.

Do this before the day rather than at the gate, where staff have no way to verify anything.

## The short version

If you are in the Windsor Enclosure at Ascot or the Best Mate or Tattersalls Enclosures at Cheltenham, wear what you like within the site wide bans on trainers, denim, leggings, shorts and novelty clothing.

If you are in the Queen Anne Enclosure, wear a suit with a standard necktie and socks over the ankle, or a dress with straps and something on your head.

If you are in the Royal Enclosure, the fascinator rule and the morning dress requirement are the two that catch people. Everything else follows from reading the published code once.

Buy your ticket from the racecourse. That is the rule most likely to actually stop you.

**Sources:** Rules on this page come from Ascot's published dress codes, picnic policy, venue policies and FAQs at ascot.com, and from the Jockey Club's Cheltenham Festival dress code guidance, checked in September 2026. Policies change between years, so check before you travel.
