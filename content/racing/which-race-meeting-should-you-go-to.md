Four meetings dominate British racing, and they suit completely different people. Royal Ascot is an occasion with a dress code. Cheltenham is the sport at its most serious. The Grand National is the race everyone has heard of. The Epsom Derby is the most relaxed of the four.

If you are going to one race meeting this year, here is how to pick.

## Which British race meeting should you go to?

Start with what you actually want from the day.

| If you want | Go to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An occasion, and you enjoy dressing up | Royal Ascot | The only meeting with an enforced dress code, in June |
| The best racing | Cheltenham Festival | The championship meeting of jump racing |
| The famous race | Grand National | The one race the whole country watches |
| The easiest first visit | Epsom Derby | No dress code, jeans and trainers explicitly fine |
| The cheapest day | Aintree or an ordinary raceday | Free parking, free children's entry |

The decision people get wrong is choosing Royal Ascot because it is the most famous, then discovering it is the only one of the four that requires clothes they do not own.

## Royal Ascot: the occasion

**When:** June, five days. **Dress code:** yes, by enclosure. **From:** £25.

Royal Ascot is a summer social occasion that happens to contain world class flat racing. The Royal Enclosure requires morning dress and a top hat; the Queen Anne Enclosure a suit and tie; the Windsor Enclosure nothing at all.

**Go if** you like the idea of dressing up, want a summer day out, and are happy for the racing to be one part of a broader occasion.

**Do not go if** you resent dress codes. Trainers, denim, leggings and shorts are banned across the entire site, including the enclosure that otherwise has no code.

The value option is genuinely good: £25 in the Windsor Enclosure, no dress code, and you can bring a picnic and a bottle of champagne.

## Cheltenham Festival: the sport

**When:** March, four days, 28 races. **Dress code:** none. **Enclosures:** Club, Tattersalls, Best Mate.

Cheltenham is where the best jump horses in Britain and Ireland meet. The crowd is there for the racing and is loud about it.

**Go if** the sport is the point, and you want to see the best horses in a discipline at their championship meeting.

**Do not go if** you feel the cold. March in the Cotswolds is genuinely hard work, and the racecourse itself advises footwear suited to conditions because of the walk from the car parks.

The four days each have a character: Champion Day, Ladies Day, St Patrick's Thursday and Gold Cup Day.

## Grand National: the famous one

**When:** April, three days at Aintree. **Dress code:** none. **Parking:** free with general admission.

The National is the race non racing people watch. Four and a quarter miles, thirty fences, and a handicap, which is why a long shot can win it.

**Go if** you want to be at the event everyone else is watching on television, in a city that treats the week as a occasion.

**Do not go if** you want a quiet afternoon. Aintree in April is a big Liverpool crowd on a big day.

Practically it is the cheapest of the four majors to attend: free on site parking with general admission, free children's entry, and no dress requirements. Fancy dress is not permitted at the Festival specifically, and team kits are discouraged.

## Epsom Derby: the easy one

**When:** June. **Dress code:** none, except the Queen Elizabeth II Stand on Derby Day.

Epsom asks racegoers to come "dressed to feel your best" and names jeans and clean trainers as a perfectly good choice. No other major British racecourse puts it that plainly.

**Go if** it is your first time at the races, or you are bringing people who would be put off by a dress code.

**Do not go if** you specifically want the dressing up part, since almost everything that makes Ascot feel like an occasion is absent here by design.

The course itself is the draw for anyone interested in the racing: Epsom Downs climbs, falls and bends left at Tattenham Corner, which makes the Derby a harder test than its distance suggests.

## Which is best for a first visit?

Epsom or an ordinary raceday, for the same reason: nothing about them requires preparation.

An ordinary Ascot raceday is the underrated option. Same course and grandstand as Royal Ascot, free parking, free children's entry, and a much lighter dress code. If you want to find out whether you enjoy racing before committing to a festival, that is the way to do it.

Royal Ascot's Windsor Enclosure is also a reasonable first visit at £25, provided you understand you will not reach the Parade Ring.

## Which is best with children?

The Grand National and ordinary racedays, on cost. Children go free at both, and parking is free at both.

Avoid the age restricted areas: Ascot's Royal Enclosure bars under 10s, and Aintree's Platinum Lounge and Princess Royal Seats are over 21s only.

At Royal Ascot, up to four children per adult get in free to the Queen Anne, Village and Windsor Enclosures, which makes it cheaper for a family than the ticket prices suggest.

## Which is best if you hate dress codes?

Anywhere except Royal Ascot. Cheltenham, Aintree and Epsom all publish guidance saying there is no formal dress code, and two of them name jeans and trainers explicitly.

Even at Royal Ascot the Windsor Enclosure has no official code, though the site wide bans on trainers and denim still apply there.

## Which is best value?

The Grand National on total cost, because free parking and free children's entry remove the two charges that inflate a day at Royal Ascot.

Royal Ascot's Windsor Enclosure is the best value single ticket at £25, especially with a picnic, since it is the only enclosure at the meeting where you can bring food and drink in.

Ordinary racedays beat all of them if you simply want to watch racing.

## The short answer

Going once, want the occasion: Royal Ascot, and read the dress code for your enclosure before buying anything.

Going once, want the racing: Cheltenham.

Going once, want the famous race: Aintree.

Going for the first time and unsure: Epsom, or an ordinary raceday at your nearest course.

**Sources:** Dress codes, enclosure structures, admission and parking details come from ascot.com and thejockeyclub.co.uk, checked in September 2026. Dates and prices are published per meeting and change between years.
