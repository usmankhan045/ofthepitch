Ascot runs around 26 racedays a year and only five of them are Royal Ascot. On the other 21, parking is free, the dress code is far lighter, children get in free, and the same racecourse costs a fraction of what it does in June.

If you want to see racing at Ascot rather than attend Royal Ascot, an ordinary raceday is a different and much cheaper proposition. Here is how they differ.

## What is the difference between Royal Ascot and an ordinary Ascot raceday?

Royal Ascot is a five day meeting in June with four enclosures, enforced dress codes, paid parking and its own ticketing. An ordinary raceday is a single afternoon of racing with two main enclosures, free parking and lighter dress requirements.

| | Ordinary raceday | Royal Ascot |
|---|---|---|
| Enclosures | King Edward VII, Queen Anne | Royal, Queen Anne, Village, Windsor |
| Parking | Free | £45 in advance |
| Gates | 10.45am or 11am by season | 10.30am |
| Children | Free under 18 | Free under 18 except Royal Enclosure |
| Buying on the day | Yes, Ticket Office East | Yes, subject to availability |

The most confusing part is that the enclosure names carry over but their status does not.

## Why is the Queen Anne Enclosure different on an ordinary raceday?

Because it changes rank. At Royal Ascot, the Queen Anne Enclosure is the premier public enclosure, with Parade Ring and Grandstand access, and it is the most expensive ticket a member of the public can buy.

On an ordinary raceday, the Queen Anne Enclosure is Ascot's **entry level** admission enclosure. The premier public enclosure on those days is the King Edward VII Enclosure.

So the same name means the top public ticket in June and the cheapest ticket in October. If you have been to Royal Ascot and book a Queen Anne ticket for an ordinary raceday expecting the same experience, that is the trap.

A third enclosure, the Winning Post Enclosure, opens on selected racedays only: King George Day, the Dubai Duty Free Shergar Cup and Qatar Racing British Champions Day.

## Is parking free at Ascot?

Yes, on every raceday except Royal Ascot. Ascot states that car parking is free of charge on all racedays during both the Flat and Jump seasons, and it cannot be pre-booked.

Car Parks 1 and 6 are usually in operation, and the car parks open at 9am. At Royal Ascot, by contrast, parking must be purchased and costs £45 in advance.

Blue Badge holders display their badge on entry and are directed to a free forward parking area.

## What time do gates open at Ascot?

It depends on the season, which is a detail almost nobody publishes.

- **Jumps season, November to February:** gates open at 10.45am, ticket offices at 10.15am.
- **Flat season, March to October:** gates open at 11am, ticket offices at 10.30am.
- **Qatar Racing British Champions Day and Royal Ascot:** gates open at 10.30am.

Race times vary by raceday rather than being fixed, and Ascot publishes them per meeting on its race times page.

## Can you buy Ascot tickets on the day?

Yes, subject to availability. Ticket Office East sells on the day and accepts both cash and card.

Ascot recommends checking the raceday page before travelling to confirm your preferred enclosure has not sold out. That is worth doing for the bigger fixtures, and rarely necessary for an ordinary midweek card.

## Can you upgrade your ticket at Ascot?

Yes. Upgrading to a more premium enclosure can be done at the Ticket Office on the day, subject to availability, or in advance by contacting the racecourse.

This is genuinely useful for a first visit. Buy the cheaper enclosure, see how the day feels, and upgrade if you want the better view rather than committing up front.

Upgrades to hospitality or a Fine Dining restaurant go through the hospitality team rather than the Ticket Office.

## Do children pay at ordinary Ascot racedays?

No. Children under 18 do not need a pre-purchased ticket and are admitted with an accompanying adult, the same as at Royal Ascot outside the Royal Enclosure.

Older teenagers may be asked for proof of identity to confirm they are under 18. Everyone over 18 needs a full adult ticket.

Combined with free parking, this makes an ordinary Ascot raceday one of the cheapest family days out available at a major sporting venue.

## Do you need a badge as well as a ticket?

Sometimes, and this catches people who assume the ticket is everything.

Every attendee needs a ticket, including members, their guests, and Fine Dining and private box guests. On top of that, badges are required for:

- **King Edward VII Enclosure ticket holders**, to reach Level 4 of the Grandstand. Posted to the lead booker.
- **Fine Dining guests**, to access their restaurant. Note that a Fine Dining badge does not grant King Edward VII Enclosure access.
- **Private box guests**, posted to the lead booker.
- **Members** holding King Edward VII, Royal Ascot Racing Club or Iron Stand membership, who need their metal pin badges on entry.

Ascot warns that badges may not be dispatched at the same time as digital tickets, so a missing badge close to the day is not necessarily a problem.

## Can someone else use a ticket booked in your name?

Yes. Ascot confirms that the lead booker's name on a ticket is a reference for tracking the booking and does not affect entry. Guests may gift their tickets to family or friends.

That is unusually relaxed compared with football or concert ticketing, and worth knowing if plans change.

## Is an ordinary Ascot raceday worth going to?

For anyone who wants to watch racing rather than attend an occasion, it is the better value proposition by a wide margin. Same course, same grandstand, same view of the same track, with free parking and no requirement to hire morning dress.

Qatar Racing British Champions Day in October is the strongest single card outside Royal Ascot, with the Winning Post Enclosure open and gates at 10.30am like the Royal meeting.

The trade off is atmosphere. An ordinary Wednesday card at Ascot is a quiet afternoon. That is either the appeal or the problem, depending on what you want from the day.

**Sources:** Enclosure structure, gate times, parking, ticketing and badge requirements are published by Ascot at ascot.com, checked in September 2026. Race times and ticket prices vary by raceday and are published per meeting.
