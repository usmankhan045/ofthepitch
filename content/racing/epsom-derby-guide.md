The Epsom Derby is the most relaxed of the big five British race meetings to attend, and the racecourse says so itself. Epsom's published guidance is to come "dressed to feel your best", and it names jeans and clean trainers as a perfectly good answer.

One exception exists. On Derby Day the Queen Elizabeth II Stand has a smart dress code, while the rest of the racecourse follows the relaxed guidance. That single distinction is the thing worth knowing before you buy a ticket.

## Is there a dress code at the Epsom Derby?

For most of the racecourse, no. Epsom Downs asks racegoers to come "dressed to feel your best" and explicitly says that for some people a sweatshirt, jeans and clean trainers is what makes them comfortable, while others prefer a sharp suit or a dress and heels.

The exception is the Queen Elizabeth II Stand on Derby Day, which does have a smart dress code in place. Epsom publishes the details separately for that stand, so check them against your ticket before the day rather than assuming the relaxed guidance covers you.

If your ticket is for anywhere else on the course, it does.

## What is not allowed at Epsom?

Two things, and neither is about formality.

**Offensive fancy dress.** Epsom permits fancy dress and says it adds colour to a raceday, but asks people to avoid inappropriate or offensive outfits. Anything deemed vulgar or derogatory may be refused entry.

**Football shirts and team kits.** Epsom asks racegoers to refrain from wearing team kits, on the basis that showing club allegiance can appear antagonistic in a mixed crowd.

That is the entire list for most of the course. There is no ban on jeans, trainers or shorts of the kind Ascot enforces site wide.

## How is Epsom different from Royal Ascot?

They sit at opposite ends of the same sport.

| | Epsom Derby | Royal Ascot |
|---|---|---|
| Dress code | "Dress to feel your best" except one stand | Enforced by enclosure, with measurements |
| Jeans | Explicitly fine | Banned site wide |
| Trainers | Clean trainers named as acceptable | Banned site wide |
| Fancy dress | Permitted if not offensive | Banned everywhere |
| Character | A day out | An occasion |

Both are flat racing. Both are historic. Ascot publishes the width of an acceptable shoulder strap; Epsom publishes a sentence about feeling comfortable.

For a first visit to the races with no suitable clothes and no wish to buy any, Epsom is the easier starting point.

## What is the Derby?

The Derby is a flat race for three year old colts and fillies run over roughly a mile and a half at Epsom Downs, and it is the most prestigious flat race in Britain. It gives its name to derbies worldwide, from the Kentucky Derby to the football fixture.

The course itself is unusual and worth understanding before you watch. Epsom Downs is not flat despite being a flat race. It climbs, then falls sharply, and bends left around Tattenham Corner before the home straight. Horses that handle the camber and the gradient win there, which is why the Derby is considered a test that a fast horse alone does not pass.

## What should you wear to the Epsom Derby?

Whatever you are comfortable standing in for a day outdoors in June, unless you are in the Queen Elizabeth II Stand on Derby Day.

If you want to dress up, plenty of people do, and it is a summer meeting so the weather usually allows it. If you would rather not, Epsom has gone further than any other major British racecourse in saying so explicitly.

The practical advice that applies either way: comfortable shoes, and something for both sun and rain, because the Downs are open ground with little shelter.

## Can you take children to the Epsom Derby?

Yes. Epsom is a family friendly meeting and the Derby Festival is one of the more accessible of the big race days for a family group, given the absence of dress requirements across most of the course.

Check current admission terms on thejockeyclub.co.uk before booking, since children's pricing is published per meeting rather than fixed year to year.

## Is the Epsom Derby worth going to?

If you have never been racing and are put off by the idea of dress codes, this is the meeting to start with. The racecourse has removed the barrier deliberately and says so in plain language on its own website.

The Derby is also the race with the most history attached to it in British flat racing, run on a course whose shape makes it genuinely different from anywhere else. You can watch the run down to Tattenham Corner and understand immediately why it is a harder race than the distance suggests.

The one thing to get right is your ticket. If you want the relaxed version of the day, avoid the Queen Elizabeth II Stand on Derby Day, since that is the one place the smart code applies.
